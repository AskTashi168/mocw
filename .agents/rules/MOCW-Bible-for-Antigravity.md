# MOCW Bible for Antigravity

## 1. Understand MOCW First

MOCW means **My One Cent Worth**.

MOCW is built around one simple identity:

> **Someone Who Listens.**

MOCW is a place where people can speak openly, think clearly and consider different perspectives without being judged or pressured.

MOCW is not therapy, counselling, coaching or a guru service.

Tashi is not a doctor or therapist. Tashi is a person who listens, asks thoughtful questions and helps people see situations more clearly.

MOCW offers perspective—not instructions.

People may consider an idea, test it for themselves and decide what is right for their own situation.

---

## 2. MOCW’s Core Philosophy

MOCW is guided by these principles:

* Nothing is absolute.
* People may see the same situation differently.
* Perspective depends on experience, knowledge, position and circumstances.
* A different perspective does not automatically mean someone is wrong.
* Facts matter.
* Logical reasoning matters.
* Experience matters.
* Perspective matters.
* Learning has no finish line.
* People can learn, adjust, ask for help and try another approach.
* The purpose is clarity, not argument.

MOCW’s guiding process is:

> **Listen • Think • Test • Grow**

---

## 3. MOCW’s Purpose

MOCW exists to help people:

* Understand themselves more clearly.
* Recognise strengths they may overlook or take for granted.
* Understand weaknesses without shame.
* Think through difficult situations.
* Consider another point of view.
* Make more informed personal decisions.
* Reduce unnecessary confusion and overthinking.
* Find possible next steps.
* Move forward with greater clarity and confidence.

MOCW should encourage progress.

The past may be acknowledged when it helps someone learn, but MOCW should not encourage unnecessary brooding over old wounds.

> **Learn from the past, but do not remain trapped in it. Move forward with clarity, patience and courage.**

---

## 4. How MOCW Should Communicate

MOCW’s tone must be:

* Human.
* Warm.
* Calm.
* Respectful.
* Thoughtful.
* Clear.
* Honest.
* Practical.
* Encouraging.
* Never arrogant or preachy.

Use simple language.

Avoid unnecessary jargon, complicated explanations, exaggerated promises, corporate language and empty motivational phrases.

Do not speak down to people.

Do not pressure people.

Do not tell people that there is only one correct way to think or act.

Use language such as:

* “You may consider…”
* “One possible way to look at this is…”
* “It may help to ask…”
* “Another perspective could be…”
* “You can decide what feels right for your situation.”

---

## 5. MOCW’s Identity

MOCW should feel:

* Personal rather than corporate.
* Thoughtful rather than loud.
* Refined rather than flashy.
* Calm rather than aggressive.
* Human rather than artificial.
* Helpful rather than sales-driven.
* Memorable rather than overloaded with information.

The central question behind every important page or article is:

> **Will someone remember this after they leave?**

---

## 6. Website Design Direction

The website must have one consistent MOCW visual identity.

All pages should feel like they belong to the same brand, while each page remains unique in its purpose, content and layout.

The design should be:

* Dark.
* Warm.
* Refined.
* Modern.
* Calm.
* Clear.
* High-quality.
* Easy to use.
* Mobile-friendly.
* Human and welcoming.

Use a shared `mocw.css` stylesheet for common design elements, including:

* Colours.
* Typography.
* Spacing.
* Navigation.
* Buttons.
* Banners.
* Cards.
* Forms.
* Footer.
* Responsive behaviour.
* Repeated page elements.

Each page may contain its own page-specific styling when necessary, but repeated styling should remain consistent.

Do not create unrelated designs for different pages.

Do not introduce major visual changes without approval.

---

## 7. Website Content Rules

Every page must have a clear purpose.

Content must be:

* Useful to real visitors.
* Easy to understand.
* Naturally written.
* Search-engine friendly.
* Helpful for AI search and understanding.
* Free from keyword stuffing.
* Free from unnecessary repetition.
* Written for people first.

Each page should answer the questions visitors are likely to have.

Important information should be easy to find.

Long pages should use clear headings and, where helpful, an “On This Page” section.

Every page should guide visitors naturally toward an appropriate next step without using pressure.

---

## 8. Website Structure

The website should support the wider MOCW purpose.

Important areas may include:

* Home.
* About.
* My Story.
* Mission and Vision.
* How I Help.
* Services.
* Pricing.
* FAQ.
* Contact.
* Articles and MOCW perspectives.
* Future knowledge and community areas.

Each page must remain focused.

Do not place every idea on every page.

Avoid duplicate content between pages.

---

## 9. Technical Working Rules

Use a simple, reliable website structure.

Prefer:

* Plain HTML.
* CSS.
* Simple JavaScript only when needed.
* A shared `mocw.css` file.
* Optimised images.
* Clear file names.
* Mobile-first layouts.
* Accessible buttons, links and forms.
* Fast-loading pages.
* Clean and readable code.

Do not add unnecessary frameworks, plugins, tools or complicated systems.

Do not make technical changes merely because they are fashionable.

The website must be easy to maintain.

---

## 10. Rules for Antigravity

Before making changes:

1. Understand the relevant page and its purpose.
2. Read the applicable MOCW rules.
3. Check the existing structure.
4. Protect working content.
5. Identify exactly what needs to change.
6. Avoid changing unrelated files.

When making changes:

* Keep the agreed MOCW identity.
* Keep pages unique but consistent.
* Use the shared `mocw.css` where appropriate.
* Do not invent new branding.
* Do not rewrite approved content without permission.
* Do not remove working features without approval.
* Do not make large changes all at once.
* Explain important changes in simple language.

Before considering work complete:

* Check desktop appearance.
* Check mobile appearance.
* Check navigation.
* Check buttons and links.
* Check images.
* Check spacing and readability.
* Check for broken pages.
* Check for obvious spelling and layout problems.
* Confirm that the page still feels like MOCW.

---

## 11. Decision-Making Rule

When unsure, choose the option that is:

1. Clearer.
2. Simpler.
3. More useful to visitors.
4. More consistent with MOCW.
5. Easier to maintain.
6. Less likely to create confusion.

Do not overcomplicate a simple task.

Do not keep revisiting settled decisions without a clear reason.

> **Build the agreed website. Keep it consistent. Make every page useful. Improve carefully. Move forward.**

---

## 12. Final MOCW Standard

Every part of MOCW should support this purpose:

> **Help people feel heard, think more clearly, understand different perspectives and move forward.**

MOCW should be useful, honest, thoughtful and memorable.

The website should not simply look good.

It should help people.
